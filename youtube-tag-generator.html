About this tool

YouTube's tag field lets you attach a set of keywords to a video that viewers never see, only YouTube's own systems read them. Tags are a minor, legacy-era ranking signal at this point, your title, description, and thumbnail do far more of the work in whether a video gets recommended or turned up in search, but filling tags in with relevant variations still costs nothing and can help YouTube place an ambiguous topic in the right context, so it's worth the two minutes.

How the tags are generated

Type a topic and this tool combines it against a bank of common modifiers (tutorial, guide, tips, for beginners, best, 2026, and more) to produce a broad set of natural-sounding variations. Add optional secondary keywords and it also pairs those directly with your topic, since real search phrasing often combines two ideas rather than one. Nothing here is pulled from YouTube's search or autocomplete data, it's template-based, so treat the result as a starting list to edit down, not a keyword-research result.

The 500-character limit

YouTube's tag field has a hard 500-character cap, counting every tag plus the commas and spaces between them. Paste in more than that and the excess gets silently cut off rather than rejected with a warning, so you could think you've added a helpful tag that YouTube never actually saved. The character count above switches to a warning color as you approach and pass that limit, remove tags with the × on each chip until you're back under it.
