How crawl budget prioritizes multiple sitemaps in robots.txt?

Crawl budget, which refers to the number of pages a search engine bot like Googlebot will crawl on your site within a given timeframe, isn't directly determined by the order or structure of sitemaps listed in your robots.txt file, but referencing multiple sitemaps there does help search engines discover and prioritize your content more efficiently. When you list multiple sitemap URLs in your robots.txt file, typically using separate lines each starting with 'Sitemap:', you're essentially giving search engines multiple entry points into your site's full URL structure, which is especially useful for large websites with different content types, like separating product pages, blog posts, and category pages into their own individual sitemaps. Google generally processes all listed sitemaps, but how much crawl budget gets allocated to each depends more on factors like your site's overall authority, server response speed, how frequently content actually changes, and internal linking structure, rather than simply the order sitemaps appear in your robots.txt file. To make the most of your crawl budget across multiple sitemaps, it helps to keep each sitemap focused and up to date, remove low-value or duplicate URLs, and ensure your most important, frequently updated content is easy for crawlers to find through strong internal linking.
