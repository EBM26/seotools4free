What is a serp?

SERP stands for Search Engine Results Page, and it refers to the page you see after typing a query into a search engine like Google, Bing, or DuckDuckGo, displaying all the results that the search engine has determined are most relevant to your search. A modern SERP is made up of much more than just a simple list of blue links, it typically includes a variety of different elements depending on the search query, such as paid advertisements at the top, organic search results, featured snippets that directly answer a question, local map packs for location-based searches, image and video carousels, People Also Ask boxes with related questions, knowledge panels providing quick facts about a topic or entity, and shopping results for product-related searches. The exact composition of a SERP changes based on what the search engine believes will best satisfy the searcher's intent, which is why SERPs for informational queries look very different from SERPs for transactional or local queries. Understanding SERP structure is fundamental to SEO because your strategy for ranking and capturing visibility depends heavily on which specific elements appear for your target keywords, since competing for a featured snippet requires a different approach than competing for a standard organic listing.
