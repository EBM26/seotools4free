Why do ai detectors flag my writing?

AI detectors sometimes flag human-written text as AI-generated for several reasons, and it can be genuinely frustrating when this happens to your own original work. These detection tools primarily analyze patterns like perplexity, meaning how predictable your word choices are, and burstiness, which measures variation in sentence length and structure, since AI-generated text has historically tended to be more uniform and statistically predictable than typical human writing. If your natural writing style happens to be clear, structured, and consistent, perhaps because you write formally, follow a specific style guide, or simply have a straightforward communication style, it can accidentally resemble the patterns these tools associate with AI output. Non-native English speakers are particularly prone to false flags, since their sentence structures sometimes appear more uniform due to how they learned English grammar rules. Heavy editing, especially using tools like Grammarly that suggest more standardized phrasing, can also push human writing toward patterns that trigger detection. It's also worth remembering that no AI detector is scientifically proven to be highly reliable, and false positive rates across the industry remain a significant, well-documented problem, which is exactly why many educators are now cautious about relying on these tools as definitive proof.
